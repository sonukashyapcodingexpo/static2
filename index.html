About the studio

Practice

Where most briefs begin.

Six starting points, with the problem each one is meant to take off a desk. The full list is grouped the way we staff the work.

01 · Custom engineering

Software Development

SapporoSys designs and builds software around a specific business, then stays close enough to support it.

Generic tools bend the company around the product. We would rather shape the product around the work.

View

02 · Android and iOS

Mobile App Development

Mobile products for field teams and customers, built for unreliable networks and everyday use.

An app that only works on the office wifi fails the moment it meets a real shift.

View

03 · Sites that sell

Website Development

Marketing sites and content platforms that load quickly and stay easy for your team to update.

A slow or brittle site quietly loses the enquiries the rest of the company is paying to earn.

View

04 · Practical automation

AI Development

Applied AI for a specific job: sorting requests, drafting replies, or reading your own documents.

A model with no boundary will sound confident about things your company never said.

View

05 · Operations software

ERP Development

ERP systems for companies that have outgrown a patchwork of sheets, chat, and desktop tools.

When finance, stock, and delivery each keep a private list, the business argues with itself.

View

06 · Multi-tenant products

SaaS Development

Subscription software with accounts, billing hooks, and a path from one customer to many.

A single-customer build is not a SaaS product until tenancy, access, and upgrades are designed.

Why this studio

Work you can put on a calendar.

The same rules on a first marketing site and on a product that already has customers.

01

Built for one operation

The software follows the work you already do.

We write the morning routine before a stack is chosen, so the system matches the desk it lands on.

  • Screens named in your words
  • Roles for the people who do the job

02

Senior people on the job

The team you meet is the team that ships.

Reviews include the engineers and designers doing the work, not a separate bench introduced after the sale.

  • The same names from workshop to release
  • No silent handoff to a new team

03

Dates in writing

Scope and risk stay visible, not implied.

A date without the risk next to it is a wish. We put both on the plan you can share.

  • A date for each phase
  • The risk that could move it

04

Tools that will still be here

We prefer supported platforms over fashion.

The stack is something your team can hire for in two years, not a framework we have to explain forever.

  • Supported platforms
  • A choice we can defend in writing

05

Care after launch

The first real month is part of the engagement.

Staff find the real edge cases in the week after go-live. That week is scheduled, not hoped for.

  • A named engineer after release
  • Small fixes inside the window

06

Yours to keep

Code, content, and accounts transfer to you.

The repository, the content, and the logins are part of the handover, not a later negotiation.

  • You hold the accounts
  • A walkthrough with your team
